# Original task implementation run (October 6)

Historical report. See [results.md](results.md) for the latest findings.


Historical run without continuous monitoring; superseded by the [monitored rerun](monitored-comparison-20261008.md).

The shared ForkJoinPool implementation completed the same one-billion-record matrix on October 6,
2026. It submits one task per index chunk and runs the four snapshot groups in the same pool.
All 225 measured snapshots and 75 warmups passed the benchmark validation checks.

`B` is `snapshotThreads`, the configured parallelism shared by index writes, hash-cache flushing,
metadata and hard-link operations. Tested values are 3, 6, 24, 48 and 96. The default of 12 was not
measured. Workload, effective settings, cache warming, source/output forcing and measurement method
match the October baseline. Force remains disabled inside the snapshot call, and hash-cache flush
overlap remains enabled.

## Snapshot times

Arithmetic mean of all nine measurements per configuration, in **seconds**. Lower is better.
No samples were discarded. [The result data](tasks-20261006.json) includes every raw sample, medians,
sample standard deviations and block means.

| Implementation | B=3 | B=6 | B=24 | B=48 | B=96 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SEGMENT | 7.865 | 7.432 | 6.530 | 6.560 | 6.634 |
| DISK | 7.663 | 7.449 | 6.582 | 6.636 | 6.728 |
| HEAP | 6.042 | 5.389 | 5.675 | 5.843 | 5.817 |
| OFF_HEAP | 7.495 | 7.468 | 6.612 | 6.621 | 6.624 |
| DISK_SEGMENT | 9.999 | 9.662 | 8.344 | 8.121 | 8.151 |

The lowest observed means are at B=24 for SEGMENT, DISK and OFF_HEAP, B=6 for HEAP, and B=48 for
DISK_SEGMENT. These are not established optimal settings. SEGMENT, OFF_HEAP and DISK_SEGMENT have
very similar means across the higher budgets, and their rankings vary between blocks. HEAP's
B=6 block means are 5.109, 5.681 and 5.377 seconds; B=24 is faster in block B.

SEGMENT/B=3 includes a 10.242-second first measurement in block A, compared with a cell median of
7.782 seconds. It remains in the reported mean.

## Historical comparison with the original baseline

This table preserves the earlier comparison with the separately generated October 2 fixture.
The [shared-fixture comparison](shared-fixture-baseline-20261007.md) superseded this comparison.

The table uses the approximate budget mapping `B = 3 × P`. It does not equate actual writer
concurrency: the baseline's other snapshot tasks run outside its writer pool, while the new pool
also performs hash flushing and other snapshot work. At P=1, baseline writes run on the calling
threads.

Change in mean snapshot time: `(task / baseline - 1) × 100`.
Positive means slower; negative means faster.

| Implementation | B=3 / P=1 | B=6 / P=2 | B=24 / P=8 | B=48 / P=16 | B=96 / P=32 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SEGMENT | +8.1% | +6.3% | +10.6% | +15.0% | +13.2% |
| DISK | -20.3% | +13.8% | +13.1% | +15.4% | +14.2% |
| HEAP | -48.1% | -9.9% | +8.2% | +11.9% | -0.2% |
| OFF_HEAP | +3.0% | +12.5% | +18.5% | +20.8% | +17.9% |
| DISK_SEGMENT | +1.7% | +12.8% | +10.9% | +8.3% | +10.3% |

At the lowest budget, DISK and HEAP improve substantially. At B=6, HEAP also improves. Those three
improvements appear in all three blocks.

At B=24/48/96, SEGMENT, DISK, OFF_HEAP and DISK_SEGMENT are generally 8–21% slower than the
corresponding baseline configurations. The same direction appears in nearly every block comparison.
HEAP is slower at B=24 and B=48; its B=96 result is effectively unchanged. The small B=3 differences
for OFF_HEAP and DISK_SEGMENT have mixed block signs and do not establish a regression.

This gives us a performance concern to investigate, but does not identify its cause. The two
campaigns ran on different days with separately generated fixtures. The task fixture occupies
about 258 GiB with 150 hash, 174 leaf and 25 bucket data files; the baseline fixture is about
262 GiB with 159 hash, 171 leaf and 27 bucket data files. Matching block labels are descriptive
comparisons, not paired trials under the same machine conditions.

The [fixed ExecutorService run](fixed-pool-20261007.md) reused the task run's saved fixture, workload and matrix.
It was generally slower than the task implementation, so it did not resolve the higher-budget
baseline concern. This comparison cannot isolate every difference from the original range-based
implementation.

## Initial attempt and successful retry

The October 5 attempt built the JAR and prepared the fixture successfully in 2h 03m 13s.
It stopped at the readiness check because swap activity was detected immediately after preparation.
No measurement blocks started. The fixture was preserved.

The October 6 retry reused that exact JAR, settings and fixture. All four frozen-input hashes match
the initial attempt. The fixture inventory matches before and after measurement, and matches the
inventory from preparation. All 29 archived-file checksums and four frozen-input checksums pass.
No application errors, benchmark failures or timeouts were found.

| Phase | Wall time |
|---|---:|
| Initial fixture preparation | 2h 03m 13s |
| Retry block A | 33m 22s |
| Retry block B | 32m 53s |
| Retry block C | 33m 04s |
| Three measurement blocks combined | 1h 39m 19s |
| Whole retry, including orchestration | 1h 39m 37s |

The readiness sample immediately before measurement showed 0.0% CPU busy, 122.7 GiB available RAM,
no swap activity, and 0.01 MiB/s disk traffic at 0.1% disk utilization. These are short samples,
not continuous machine monitoring.

Forcing output files after snapshot return took about 8.0 seconds on average, including warmups,
and remains outside the timed score. The shorter whole campaign is not itself evidence of faster
snapshot calls: it also includes source setup, forcing, validation and cleanup.
