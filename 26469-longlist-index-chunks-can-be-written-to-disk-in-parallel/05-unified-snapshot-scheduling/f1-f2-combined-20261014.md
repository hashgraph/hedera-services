# Combined pre-sizing and bounded chains — October 14, 2026

[Latest summary](results.md) · [All measurements and calculated comparisons](f1-f2-combined-20261014.json)

## Result

Tasks + F1 + F2 is close to baseline + F1: its mean snapshot times range from 2.5% faster to
1.3% slower across all five index implementations. It removes most of the earlier gap, especially
for HEAP, but does not establish that shared-pool tasks are faster than the pre-sized baseline.

Only the combined task variant ran today. All comparison values below come from
[October 13](f1-f2-20261013.md). The same fixture and exact task JAR were reused, but the controls
were not rerun. Small differences can reflect run-to-run conditions as well as the code change.

## Does combining the fixes help?

These columns compare the combined version with the earlier task variants, not with the baseline.

| Index implementation | vs tasks without fixes | vs tasks + F1 | vs tasks + F2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| SEGMENT | -18.7% | -3.9% | -6.1% |
| DISK | -20.4% | -0.8% | -6.6% |
| HEAP | -16.1% | -11.6% | -5.2% |
| OFF_HEAP | -20.3% | -3.9% | -7.8% |
| DISK_SEGMENT | -17.3% | +2.6% | -3.4% |

HEAP is the clearest additional improvement over tasks + F1. Its baseline-F1 gap falls from
14.5% to 1.2%. SEGMENT and OFF_HEAP also improve on mean and median. DISK changes little,
while DISK_SEGMENT has a higher mean than F1 alone. The fixes' separate gains must not be added
together, and this cross-day comparison cannot isolate their interaction or the underlying cause.

All three new HEAP, SEGMENT and OFF_HEAP fork means are below their October 13 task-F1 overall
means. This is descriptive consistency, not a paired or significance test. No samples were removed.

## Machine activity

Sampling covered all 15 cells: 231 process/CPU reports, 232 memory/swap reports and 232 disk
reports. Gaps were at most six seconds. Initial historical CPU/vmstat reports were excluded.

- At least 111.6 GiB RAM remained available. No swap-out; one 1 KiB/s swap-in interval.
- At most two Java processes appeared together, consistent with the launcher and benchmark fork.
- Periodic `cf-agent` activity reached 70.3% of one core, about 2.2% of the 32-logical-CPU host.
  This is background activity, not a benchmark writer.
- A resource-only screen at 2% of one core found three possible measured-snapshot overlaps:
  block A SEGMENT measurement 2, block A DISK_SEGMENT measurement 1, and block B SEGMENT
  measurement 1. Five-second CPU windows overlap their logged snapshot intervals; the active
  sample timestamps fall after them. This does not prove when the CPU work happened or its impact.
- No application warnings/errors or benchmark failures were found. Console warnings were the
  standard JMH Unsafe deprecation notices.

There is no sampled evidence of sustained competing load or memory pressure, but this was not
a perfectly isolated host. Sampling cannot attribute all I/O or exclude short-lived interference.
All measurements are retained. Monitoring supports using the run; it does not remove the
cross-day limitation when judging differences of a few percent.
